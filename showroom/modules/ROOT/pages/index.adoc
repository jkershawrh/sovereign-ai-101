== The situation

Your company wants to use an AI assistant with internal information. A useful answer is not enough. Before approving the workload, the platform team must answer four questions:

. What kind of request is this?
. Which policy allowed or blocked it?
. Did generation actually run?
. Can another reviewer verify the evidence?

In this lab, you are the platform reviewer. You will send four requests through the same governed boundary and see how the system behaves when the request is ordinary, sensitive, hostile, or impossible to authorize.

== What you will do

[cols="1,3,1",options="header"]
|===
|Step |Hands-on outcome |Time
|1 |Frame the business decision |2 min
|2 |Create an isolated governed session |3 min
|3 |Run general and sensitive requests |7 min
|4 |Prove an injection is blocked before generation |4 min
|5 |Make policy unavailable and observe fail-closed behavior |4 min
|6 |Retrieve and verify correlated evidence |5 min
|7 |Make the human approval decision |3 min
|8 |Delete the session and local artifacts |2 min
|===

You will leave with a small decision brief containing the request classification, policy result, execution status, evidence status, and accountable human role.

== The architecture you are testing

[source,text]
----
Participant request
        |
        v
Classification rules --> Policy decision
        |                     |
        +-------- allow ------+----> bounded generation
        |                     |
        +---- deny/unknown ---+----> no generation
                              |
                              v
                       evidence receipts
                              |
                              v
                         human review
----

The important lesson is separation of duties: policy grants permission, the model produces text, the ledger preserves evidence, and a person accepts or rejects the result.
